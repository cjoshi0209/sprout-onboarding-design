5. Accessibility notes

  • Real buttons, links, inputs and labels everywhere, so keyboard and screen readers work without extra roles.
  • Every field has a visible label, with its “why we ask” line directly beneath.
  • Errors are sentences, never colour alone, and announced as alerts.
  • Verification status is text first and announced politely as it changes.
  • Progress is shown as bars and as words (“3 of 5”).
  • 3 px focus ring at 6.99:1. Motion stops under reduced motion.

6. Hindi readiness

Nothing has a fixed width, so labels, buttons and cards grow with their text and wrap. Body line height is 1.6 and headings 1.2 or more, which keeps Devanagari matras from clipping. The font stack ends in Noto Sans Devanagari so Hindi lands in matching weights. Copy is plain strings, ready to move to a translation file. A sample is on the Type and components board; it still needs a native-speaker review and a visual test at 30% longer strings.

7. Testing the copy against the support complaints

“I didn’t know I needed PAN and Aadhaar.”The homepage says to keep them handy, Welcome lists all three items, and screen 2 is a tick-off checklist with a real “Remind me later”. A first-time user knows what to prepare before they begin.
“I thought the app crashed.”Screen 4 always names its state, shows which check is running, gives an honest range (“usually 2 to 5 minutes, sometimes a few hours”), says there is nothing to do, and offers a notification. Loading, slow, error and empty states are all designed.

8. My three biggest risks

1. Verification time is not ours to controlIf a check takes hours, good messaging cannot rescue every user. Mitigation: notify-me with a real SMS or push, and an honest range from the verification provider. Needs a backend commitment.
2. Minimal may under-explainCutting the video, ticker and pop-ups removes noise, but some visitors may want more proof before they trust a new app. Mitigation: measure bounce against the 78% baseline and add a light “how it works” first.
3. Regulatory wording is a draftThe disclaimer, risk note and sample fund text are plain-language drafts. Compliance must approve the exact wording and the PAN and Aadhaar flow. The sample fund is illustrative, not a recommendation.
Assumptions to confirm with engineering and compliance: the 10-minute and 2 to 5 minute estimates, the four verification steps, the OTP flow, and the reminder channel (SMS). The Figma file with a clickable prototype is a separate deliverable.
