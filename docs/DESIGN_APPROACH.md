# Brief design approach (text for the submission form)

I designed the homepage and the first five onboarding screens as one story: the user starts at "this is scary" and ends at "I've got this". Each screen does one job and removes one fear. The homepage makes one promise (start from ₹100, at your own pace) with one button. Screens 1 and 2 tell people exactly what to have ready (PAN, Aadhaar-linked mobile, bank account) and give a real "Remind me later", which answers the support complaint about not knowing what to prepare. Screen 3 explains why each field is asked. Screen 4 always names the verification state, says what is happening, gives an honest time range and offers a notification, which answers the "it crashed" complaint; loading, slow, error and empty states are all designed. Screen 5 starts at the ₹100 minimum, lets the user pick the amount, and states the risk in plain language.

The mascot is a minimal geometric sprout (a disc, soil, a stem and two leaves) with no face or limbs, so it reads as a premium brand mark rather than a cartoon. It grows with the user (seed, sprout, two leaves, a small bud when done), sways slowly with CSS only, and falls back to a static pose. It is also the logo, so it links the homepage to the last screen. I resolved the stakeholder conflict by keeping the mascot as a mark and cutting the ticker, pop-ups and homepage video; the referral moves to one low-priority line after the first investment.

Accessibility: every colour pair meets WCAG AA (lowest text pair 5.62:1), tap targets are 44 px or more, controls are real buttons, links and inputs with visible labels, errors are sentences, and motion stops under reduced motion. A 12 px risk disclaimer is pinned and visible on every mobile screen. Performance: no images, one font family, inline SVG and CSS-only motion, designed for low-end phones on 4G (not yet measured in a build). Hindi readiness: wrapping labels, no fixed widths, Devanagari-friendly line heights and a Noto Sans Devanagari fallback.
